Photo essay · Fortress Malta · Part 7

Grand Harbour at first light

Eleven photographs from a boat, taken in the hour the convoy crews would have seen it. Words by James, between the pictures.

Friday 19 March 2027·11 photographs · 6 minFree this week12 commentsJames replied
Photo 1 · 16:9 · full bleed
The breakwater from the water, 5:40am. The Ohio came in past here on 15 August 1942, held up by two destroyers lashed alongside.WW2 & All That · 2026

You cannot understand Pedestal from the land. From Valletta the harbour is a view; from a small boat at dawn it is a trap with one way in, and every gun on both sides of it pointing at you.

We went out at five with a fisherman from Marsaxlokk who has done this run for forty years. He did not need the map.

Photo 2 · 3:2
Photo 3 · 3:2
Left: Fort St Elmo from the north. Right: the same fort from the harbour mouth, where the Italian E-boats came in on 26 July 1941 and did not come out.WW2 & All That

The thing the photographs cannot give you is the noise. In 1942 this water would have been full of it: the Bofors on the bastions, the dive-bombers, the dockyard sirens. At 5:40 on a Tuesday in October it is gulls and a diesel.

Photo 4 · 2:3 · portrait
The entrance to Dockyard Creek. The Ohio was towed in here, settling, and unloaded with pumps running until she sat on the bottom.WW2 & All That
Photo 5 · 16:9 · full bleed
Looking back at Valletta from Senglea Point as the sun comes up. This is the view the Pedestal survivors had, and the one the island’s population had of them.WW2 & All That

There is a famous photograph of the crowds on these bastions as the Ohio came in. We stood where the photographer stood. The bastion is the same; the crowd is a coach party from Bremen, and they are also cheering, at a cruise ship.

Six more pictures follow, and then the map pack has the convoy track laid over this exact stretch of water.
